\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\thispagestyle{plain}

\noindent Endoscopic polyp segmentation is a clinically important task for
computer-aided colonoscopy, but it remains challenging because polyps vary in
scale, texture, boundary clarity, and imaging domain. This partial thesis
reports a current-stage reproduction and benchmarking study of SAM-family
models for promptable polyp segmentation. The work establishes a reproducible
v3 evaluation protocol using the held-out Kvasir-SEG validation split, the
cross-domain CVC-ClinicDB dataset, a fixed $\pm30$ pixel box-prompt setting,
and five metrics: Dice, IoU, precision, recall, and HD95. Under this protocol,
the thesis evaluates zero-shot SAM and SAM2 backbone scaling, generic medical
pre-training transfer through MedSAM, PP-SAM 1/5/10-shot prompt-perturbation
fine-tuning, and SAMed LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning. ASPS is reported separately as an external fully supervised
upper-bound reference, while OP-SAM is reported as a CPG-only exploratory
training-free pilot. The results show that larger backbones do not monotonically
improve zero-shot polyp segmentation, generic medical pre-training does not
guarantee better transfer to colonoscopy, PP-SAM provides the strongest K-shot
cross-domain performance, and SAMed LoRA becomes competitive only when trained
on the full Kvasir training split. The thesis concludes by identifying the next
alignment tasks required for the long-term project: ASPS-v3 retraining, full
OP-SAM reproduction, polyp-specific Medical SAM Adapter training, prompt-quality
ablation, object-scale analysis, weak-boundary refinement, and extension to
additional datasets.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Medical Image Segmentation, Segment Anything Model
(SAM), Promptable Foundation Models, Endoscopic Polyp Segmentation,
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning, Cross-Domain Generalization
