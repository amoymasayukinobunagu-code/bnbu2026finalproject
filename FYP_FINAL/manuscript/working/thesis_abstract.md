Endoscopic polyp segmentation requires a model to delineate lesions that vary in size, appearance and boundary contrast. Promptable segmentation models can use location cues supplied as boxes or points, but their performance depends on how those cues are generated and whether the model has been adapted to the target task. This study evaluates general-purpose Segment Anything Model (SAM), medically pretrained MedSAM and polyp-specific adaptations under a shared prompting protocol.

The primary comparison used 880 Kvasir-SEG images for adaptation, 120 Kvasir-SEG validation images and 612 CVC-ClinicDB frames for evaluation. The reference prompt was a bounding box derived from the annotation and expanded by 30 pixels on each side. The comparison included low-rank adaptation, Medical SAM Adapter and PP-SAM as an existing fine-tuning reference. Segmentation was assessed using Dice, intersection over union and contour metrics. A separate benchmark measured inference latency on three configurations.

SAM ViT-L achieved the highest zero-shot mean Dice on Kvasir-SEG validation and CVC-ClinicDB, at 0.8140 and 0.6588, respectively. All four full-data low-rank or adapter configurations improved mean Dice over their corresponding zero-shot baselines on both primary datasets. PP-SAM achieved the highest mean Dice on Kvasir-SEG validation (0.9226), while SAMed led on CVC-ClinicDB (0.8608). In the five-configuration external follow-up, PP-SAM led on CVC-ColonDB and zero-shot SAM ViT-B led on ETIS-LaribPolypDB. The effects of prompt type, translation and enlargement varied by configuration and dataset. Area and boundary-contrast analyses also revealed configuration-dependent differences.

The study provides a reproducible reference for comparing promptable polyp segmentation under controlled location cues. The results characterise segmentation of individual frames using annotation-derived prompts and illustrate why cross-dataset evaluation is needed when selecting a model.
