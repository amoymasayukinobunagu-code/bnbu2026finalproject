Promptable Endoscopic Polyp Segmentation Under a Common Box
Protocol
[Author names and affiliations]

Abstract

This study compares released Segment Anything Model (SAM) and MedSAM checkpoints with low-
rank and adapter-based adaptations for endoscopic polyp segmentation. Adaptation used 880 Kvasir-
SEG images. Evaluation used 120 Kvasir-SEG validation images and 612 CVC-ClinicDB frames,
with boxes derived from the reference masks and expanded by 30 pixels. SAM ViT-L led the zero-
shot configurations on both datasets. All four full-data LoRA or adapter configurations improved
mean Dice over their corresponding zero-shot baselines. PP-SAM led on Kvasir validation at 0.9226
and SAMed led on CVC-ClinicDB at 0.8608. A five-configuration follow-up showed different
rankings on CVC-ColonDB and ETIS-LaribPolypDB. Prompt and subgroup analyses showed that
the observed gains depended on the prompt, model and dataset.

1 Introduction

A spatial prompt guides a segmenter towards a lesion, while the model must infer the extent of that
lesion from the image. SAM [1] and MedSAM [2] provide general and medical-domain starting
points. SAMed [3] and Medical SAM Adapter [4] offer ways to adapt these checkpoints, while PP-
SAM [5] uses perturbed training boxes. We compare released and polyp-adapted checkpoints using
the same location information. We also test whether model rankings persist across datasets and
whether results change under different prompts and image conditions.

2 Methods

The 880-image training and 120-image validation partitions came from Kvasir-SEG [6]. CVC-
ClinicDB [7] supplied 612 evaluation frames and did not participate in checkpoint selection. The
zero-shot group comprised SAM ViT-B, SAM ViT-L and MedSAM. The full-data group included
rank-four SAMed, rank-four MedSAM+LoRA, Medical SAM Adapter with ViT-B and ViT-L, and
PP-SAM. The LoRA configurations updated the low-rank matrices, prompt encoder and mask
decoder. The adapter configurations updated inserted image-encoder modules and the mask decoder.
PP-SAM updated the image and prompt encoders with a frozen mask decoder.
The default box was the foreground bounding rectangle expanded by 30 pixels and clipped to the
native frame bounds. Predictions were restored to native resolution before scoring. We averaged Dice
and intersection over union (IoU) per image. Surface HD95 was the larger of the two directed 95th
percentiles of nearest-boundary distance, with empty predictions excluded from this metric. We also
evaluated fixed box expansions, point prompts and constrained box translations. The subgroup
analysis used the fraction of annotated foreground pixels and the difference between the mean colours
inside and outside the annotated boundary.

3 Results

Table 1 summarises the primary comparison. SAM ViT-L gave the highest zero-shot Dice on both datasets. PP-SAM
gave the lowest mean surface HD95 on both, at 41.58 pixels on Kvasir-SEG and 22.78 pixels on CVC-ClinicDB.

The five-configuration follow-up used 342 screened CVC-ColonDB images [8] and 190 ETIS-
LaribPolypDB images [9]. All four adapted configurations outscored SAM-B on CVC-ColonDB,
where PP-SAM led at 0.8493 Dice. On ETIS-LaribPolypDB, SAM-B led at 0.8613, followed by PP-
SAM at 0.8436. The 50-pixel expansion gave lower Dice than 0 and 10 pixels in every verified
expansion series. With an interior point on CVC-ClinicDB, mean Dice increased from 0.5182 to
0.6305 for SAM-B and from 0.7685 to 0.8068 for MSA-B. With the same point strategy on Kvasir,
SAM-B's Dice fell from 0.7497 to 0.6840. On CVC-ClinicDB, SAM-B's Dice declined from 0.5573
to 0.2295 between the small and large area groups, while PP-SAM's rose from 0.7970 to 0.8986.

4 Discussion and conclusion

The gains from full-data adaptation on the primary datasets were configuration- and dataset-
dependent. The change in ranking on ETIS-LaribPolypDB motivates testing candidate models on
more than one source. The comparisons reflect each model's training, preprocessing and decoder
settings. Validation scores also reflect checkpoint selection. The default box supplies target location
from the reference annotation, so the main results describe segmentation with a known location cue.
